## Purpose and process boundary

Radiation and heat changed the organic into gases, lower-boiling products and heavier material. Degassing controlled gas/volatile content, while condensation recovered selected material from the tank vapour stream. Vapour pressure depended on dissolved gases and volatile concentration as well as temperature. Removing every light product was not necessarily the minimum-consumption strategy: early work indicated that retained or recycled volatiles could undergo further reactions to useful higher-boiling coolant. [AECL-2553, p. 4](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4528366#page=5); [AECL-2943, p. 4](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=8).

## Operating development

In June 1967 the WR-1 coolant had not reached the assumed steady composition. The authors attributed higher-than-predicted volatile losses partly to condensers designed to operate at high temperature. Condenser modifications and a later equilibrium composition were expected to reduce coolant consumption. The published 2.5–2.8 kg/MWd target was an expectation for the combined future conditions, not a demonstrated condenser acceptance test. [AECL-2943, p. 4](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=8).

The same source linked increasing high-boiler concentration to changing decomposition rates. Consequently, a before/after condenser comparison would require coolant composition and irradiation conditions as well as temperature. An apparent reduction in makeup cannot be assigned to condenser performance alone without that accounting.

## Radioactivity and connected services

Ordinary organic decomposition gas and fission gas from failed fuel are different source terms. In the major early experimental-fuel failure, purging removed fission gases but substantial radiation fields remained on primary piping. Particulate removal then helped reduce the residual contamination. Gas handling therefore did not remove every radionuclide-bearing form. [AECL-3523, §9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=15).

The EIS separately lists relief exhaust, nitrogen supply and active ventilation. The recovered descriptions do not provide the complete routing from each condenser outlet through these systems to final treatment or release. A complete off-gas diagram cannot be inferred from the equipment list alone. [CNL 2022, p. 3-16](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=156).

## Remaining data

Missing items include gas and condensate flow rates, condenser cooling-water duties, pressure drops, noncondensable-gas composition, recycle fractions, tank separation arrangements, activity-monitor locations and release controls. Condensation removes condensable species; no unsupported claim of noble-gas capture or quantified radiological decontamination is made. The source evidence establishes process purpose and equipment scale, not a complete emissions balance.
