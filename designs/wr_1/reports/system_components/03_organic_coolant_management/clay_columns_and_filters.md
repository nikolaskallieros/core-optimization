## Process path

Approximately 15% of the degassing return flow went through particulate-removal equipment. This denominator matters: the source does not say 15% of total main-circuit flow. Coolant passed through a cooler, an attapulgus clay adsorption column and a backup filter. Two columns were installed, with one column and a filter normally in service. The stated inlet temperature to the columns was 300°C. [CNL 2020, §2.5](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=9).

## Adsorption and filtration have different roles

The clay treatment addressed fouling substances; downstream filters retained particles, including clay that might escape its basket. Neither process should be equated with removal of all dissolved high boilers. Inference: cooler performance, flow and contact time influence the treatment seen by the coolant, but the sources do not quantify an adsorption isotherm, capacity or breakthrough criterion.

Early comparison between the two WR-1 circuits suggested that clay was useful for cleaning contaminated coolant but showed little benefit once coolant was already clean. The 1967 account similarly considered whether filters alone might suffice for extended clean operation. These were qualified observations during development, not a demonstration that clay treatment was unnecessary for every later condition. [AECL-2553, pp. 4–5](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4528366#page=5); [AECL-2943, §2.4](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=9).

## Commissioning carryover

The original baskets were open at the top. During initial introduction of the columns, baskets floated and apparent reverse flow carried clay out. Most was caught by the glass-wool filters. The remedy added screens above the clay and springs to keep the baskets seated. This happened before fuel installation, and the filters removed the clay from the circuit. [AECL-2552, §2.2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=7).

The incident demonstrates why retention mechanics and transient flow direction matter as well as steady filtration. It does not establish a permissible later carryover concentration or prove every valve lineup was resistant to reversal.

## Contamination service and limitations

After a severe experimental-fuel rupture, primary piping remained highly radioactive after fission-gas purging. Operation of the particulate-removal system accelerated the apparent decline of piping activity; reactor operation resumed after roughly six weeks in that account. This supports a cleanup role for the system but does not measure a universal radionuclide-removal factor. [AECL-3523, §9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=15).

Bed mass, flow rate, pressure-drop limit, replacement interval, clay regeneration, waste activity and filtration efficiency versus particle size remain missing. C's shared cooler also creates an equipment dependency between treatment and standby cooling, but no conflicting-duty event or quantitative loss of capability is established by the equipment list.
