## Biological-shield envelope

The reactor vault walls used heavy concrete to attenuate radiation from the core. Heavy concrete also occurred near the upper/lower access spaces and shutdown shields. This bulk shield was distinct from the actively cooled thermal shields closer to the calandria and from the movable top-deck shielding. [CNL 2020, §2.3](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=8).

## Concrete-cooling equipment

The EIS separately lists two circulation pumps, a U-tube heat exchanger, a head/degassing tank, six cooling coils, piping and instrumentation for the concrete-cooling system. It does not provide coil locations, dimensions, branch flow rates or allowable concrete temperatures. [CNL 2022, p. 3-15](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=155).

The two pumps should not automatically be assigned the same operating logic as the thermal-shield pumps. The latter are explicitly described as one running and one standby; that statement is not separately provided for the concrete loop in the reviewed passage. Likewise, a shared equipment room does not establish a common water circuit.

## Penetrations and local performance

Heavy-water and helium pipes and the reactor-vault exhaust duct passed through the concrete. Three ion-chamber penetrations are identified. Such openings create geometry-specific radiation paths. During high-power commissioning, overall shielding was judged adequate except for narrow beams reaching 300 mR/h from the bottoms of ion-chamber housings. [CNL 2022, p. 3-5](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=145); [AECL-2552, §5](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=10).

The observation illustrates a local limitation despite substantial bulk shielding. It is not evidence that all adjacent areas had that field or that the 60-MW design objective was verified everywhere during the smaller-core commissioning programme.

## Thermal and lifetime interpretation

Inference: cooling limits temperature rise and associated gradients in concrete surrounding the reactor. Establishing cracking or strength margins would require the concrete formulation, moisture condition, restraint, deposited heat and temperature histories. Density alone cannot supply those properties or a shielding attenuation coefficient.

The later characterization report treats activated concrete and retained cooling water as decommissioning concerns. Its combined estimate of up to 21 m³ of remaining water in thermal-shield and concrete systems is not a concrete-loop operating inventory. [CNL 2020, §6.8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=28).

No original shielding calculation, full reinforcement/penetration drawing set, temperature-monitor map or lifetime concrete inspection record was recovered. The available evidence supports the broad shield envelope and cooling arrangement, while local radiation and structural performance require more specific information.
