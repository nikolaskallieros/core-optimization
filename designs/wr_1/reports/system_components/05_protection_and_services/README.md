WR-1's protective functions included terminating fission, maintaining fuel cooling, detecting failed boundaries, controlling fire and contamination, and supplying essential equipment. Moderator dumping belonged to the reactivity system; it did not replace pump rundown, coolant injection or a heat sink. The following reports separate these functions and the evidence available for each.

## Different events require different functions

| Event | Documented relevant provision | What the equipment description alone does not prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loss of a pump drive | Flywheel rundown | Adequate flow for a specified duration. |
| Organic loss from a pipe break | Pressurized fresh-organic injection connected at inlet and outlet locations | Required injection rate and successful coverage of every break. |
| Fuel defect | Coolant activity monitoring | Reliable identification at all backgrounds and for every fuel type. |
| Hot organic spill | Leak/smoke detection, sprays, local CO₂ prevention and drainage | Absence of ignition or damage to essential equipment. |
| Loss of normal AC | Standby generation and DC battery support | Complete load coverage, transfer time or battery autonomy. |

The provisions are described in [CNL 2022, pp. 3-9 and 3-15–3-16](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=149) and the early operating sources. The right-hand column identifies missing performance evidence, not a finding that the systems failed.

One important observed dependency was detector background: experimental-fuel rupture could contaminate a primary circuit enough to prevent the failed-fuel system from functioning until cleanup. Another was ventilation-filter loading by organic vapour. Low ordinary coolant activity therefore did not eliminate monitoring and contamination-control requirements. [AECL-3523, §§7, 9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=13).

The recovered material does not constitute the original operating safety case or a complete single-failure assessment. Retrospective statements about equipment remaining after decommissioning are not descriptions of operating-era redundancy.
