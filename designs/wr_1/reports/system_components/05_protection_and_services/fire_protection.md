## Hazard and prevention arrangement

WR-1 circulated combustible organic at temperatures near or above reported autoignition conditions. A leak's consequences depended on surfaces, insulation, residence time, oxygen and ventilation as well as bulk coolant temperature. The upper channel-closure space had many potential leak locations and was one of the confined hot spaces discussed explicitly in the operating account. [AECL-3523, §5](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=11).

| Provision | Documented purpose or location |
| --- | --- |
| Fog and water sprays | Protection of rooms containing organic circuits. |
| CO₂ systems in selected hot spaces | Reduce oxygen where adequate insulation or ventilation was impractical. |
| Explosion-proof electrical fittings and grounded piping | Reduce ordinary ignition sources. |
| Carefully installed insulation | Limit air spaces and vulnerability to organic wetting. |
| Ventilation | Reduce local air temperature and organic-vapour concentration where practical. |
| Leak/smoke detection and alarms | Detect abnormal conditions. |
| Organic drainage | Collect spills from rooms containing organic piping/equipment. |

Sources: [AECL-3523, pp. 6–8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=11); [CNL 2022, pp. 3-15–3-16](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=155). Selected-space CO₂ protection is not a claim that the whole building was inerted.

## Autoignition numbers require their source context

| Source | Reported observations | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1967 technology account | WR-1 coolant autoignition temperature reportedly rose from 390°C to 425°C as composition evolved | Test method and mixture history must accompany use of the values. |
| 1969 operating account | About 350°C with no high boilers to 395°C at 30% high boilers | A different published range, not silently averaged with the earlier one. |
| 1969 laboratory discussion | Ignition on hot piping depended on sufficient contact time, about ten seconds in the described tests, and surrounding air temperature | Not a universal ignition-delay correlation. |

Sources: [AECL-2943, pp. 4–5](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=8); [AECL-3523, p. 6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=11). These values do not establish one plant-wide safe operating temperature.

## Documented events and responses

In April 1966 a maintenance spill soaked insulation in the lower access space, followed by autoignition. The account reports easy extinguishment and negligible damage; the insulation was redesigned to reduce vulnerability to spills. The same source mentions other small glowing-insulation fires. It does not support a “no fires” claim. [AECL-3523, §§5, 9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=15).

Two valve-component failures released substantial organic at about 370°C without ignition. One involved an overstressed clamp bolt and the other a deficient valve-stem stop; redesign followed. These events show that hot release did not inevitably ignite, while the insulation event shows that absence of ignition in one release does not bound another. [AECL-3523, §9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=15).

## Drainage and remaining evidence

Organic-room floor drains led to the organic sump, with transfer available to the active-drainage system. Drainage could limit local accumulation but also moved potentially contaminated liquid toward waste-handling equipment. The public descriptions do not establish fire-flow capacity, drain sealing under a spill, suppression-water inventories or all possible overflow routes. [CNL 2022, p. 3-15](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=155).

No complete operating fire-hazards analysis, separation distances, CO₂ concentration/time specification, nozzle coverage calculation or essential-equipment survivability assessment was recovered. The retrospective inventory's statement that some systems were later shut down concerns decommissioning status, not their availability while the reactor operated.
