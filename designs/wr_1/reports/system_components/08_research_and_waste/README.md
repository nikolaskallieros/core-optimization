WR-1 could replace a main-circuit fuel-channel connection with an independently circulated experimental loop. The later described facilities comprised water loop 1L2, three organic fast-neutron loops and an external hydraulic loop. Independent test-section circulation did not mean absence of shared coolant-conditioning, emergency, electrical or heat-rejection services. [CNL 2020, §2.9](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11); [CNL 2022, p. 3-9](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=149).

## Experimental scope

| Facility | What its evidence can establish | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1L2 water loop | Behaviour under selected pressurized or boiling water conditions | Its coolant, purification and emergency inventory differ from organic loops. |
| 1L4/1L5/1L6 | Fuel/material behaviour in an organic-cooled fast-flux test region | Experiment-specific flux, load and hydraulic conditions are required for quantitative transfer. |
| 1L1 | Full-size channel/assembly hydraulic behaviour with electric heating | It cannot directly reproduce irradiation-driven chemistry or in-core material damage. |
| Waste systems | Documented collection and treatment paths | Equipment existence does not establish removal efficiency or zero historical release. |

The first column identifies documented facilities. The transfer limits are engineering interpretations of their different environments. The early design's provision for “at least five” loops is not the same as the later inventory of four in-reactor loops plus one external facility. [65-CNA-205, §4.1.2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=7); [CNL 2020, §2.9](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

The drainage reports distinguish historical process-water discharge from later active-liquid treatment descriptions. Neither the original operating permits nor a complete revision-by-revision drainage map was recovered, so the 2022 system should not be projected unchanged back to 1965.
