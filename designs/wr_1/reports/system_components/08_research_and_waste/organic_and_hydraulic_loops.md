## Fast-neutron loop purpose and fuel

The three organic loops supported tests of creep and other irradiation-driven material changes, and development of fuels and channels. Each was described as designed to remove 4.5 MWth. CNL identifies 4.95-wt% enriched UC elements arranged in a ring around a large central support tube carrying experiments. That geometry is separate from the main-core driver bundle. [CNL 2020, §§2.7, 2.9.4](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=10).

Table 1 associates the fast-neutron facilities with lattice positions D-9, F-9 and E-6. The source passage lists the group and positions; this report does not invent a one-to-one loop-to-position assignment beyond that wording. The system descriptions place associated equipment in loop rooms near the reactor. [CNL 2020, Table 1](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=33).

## Shared coolant-conditioning interface

A small outlet bleed connected with A/B degassing and pressure control; conditioned coolant returned from A/B degassing pumps, with the loop pressurizing pump feeding circulation-pump suction. Separate loop circulation therefore coexisted with a material connection to the main-circuit auxiliaries. [CNL 2022, p. 3-9](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=149).

Inference: this connection can exchange chemical species and, following a fuel defect, mobile activity. The topology alone does not determine transfer rate, which circuit received a particular release or whether isolation prevented it. The 2020 characterization report describes contamination of loops and associated degassing/particulate equipment after fuel failures, but individual events require their own routing records. [CNL 2020, §6.3.1](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=23).

## External hydraulic loop 1L1

1L1 was a separate, out-of-reactor facility for full-size channels and fuel assemblies. Its equipment comprised a circulation pump, pressurizing pump, three test sections, three electric heaters, makeup/degassing tank, condenser circuit, purification circuit and loop cooler. [CNL 2020, §2.9.2](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

| Test-section feature | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Inner structure | WR-1 fuel channel. |
| Outer structure | Insulated 6-in carbon-steel pipe jacket supported from the building. |
| Annulus | CO₂ insulation between channel and jacket. |
| End seals | Similar in arrangement to channel/calandria-extension seals. |
| Branch layout | Three parallel sections between the reactor-hall hatch and Room 410. |
| Feeder adaptation | Variable outlet connections accommodated different WR-1 channel elevations. |

Source: [CNL 2020, p. 12](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=12). The 6-in pipe description is not a recovered inside diameter or tolerance; no annulus clearance is calculated from it.

## What can and cannot transfer

1L1 could support hydraulic and electrically heated testing without the in-core radiation field. Inference: it could not directly reproduce radiolytic coolant evolution, irradiation creep or the fast-neutron exposure of 1L4/1L5/1L6. Conversely, a successful irradiation did not by itself identify all pressure-loss or vibration mechanisms without measurements.

The recovered sources omit detailed loop operating envelopes, pump curves, specimen dimensions, fast-flux spectra, heat-exchanger duties, irradiation histories and emergency-flow acceptance results. The 4.5-MWth value is a stated design heat-removal capacity per fast-neutron loop, not evidence that every experiment operated at that power.
