## Converting a reactor site to a loop

A loop used a fuelled test section in a reactor lattice position, connected to equipment and instruments in a nearby room. Its inlet/outlet feeders were disconnected from the main heat-transport circuit and connected to the loop piping. The reactor supplied the irradiation environment while the separate loop established selected flow, pressure and temperature conditions. [CNL 2020, §2.9.1](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

The 1965 design emphasized the ability to test different coolants, pressure channels and materials. Its broad planned capability is not evidence that every proposed coolant or loop was eventually installed. The later inventory identifies four in-reactor loops, of which 1L2 was water-cooled. [65-CNA-205, §4.1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=7); [CNL 2020, §2.9](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

## 1L2 duties and modification

Located primarily in Room 539, 1L2 was intended to operate in pressurized-water or boiling-water modes. The 2020 account describes a 1974 modification for Bruce and later CANDU conditions, including activity-transport studies in two-phase flow. This was an irradiation and system-research facility, not another ordinary organic circuit. [CNL 2020, §2.9.3](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=12).

## Equipment and flow functions

| Subsystem | Documented components and role |
| --- | --- |
| Main circulation | Distilled light water through the test section using one of two pumps. |
| Inlet-temperature control | Five immersion heaters upstream of the test section. |
| Pressure/phase management | Surge tank and separator vessel; separator removed steam from outlet flow when the boiler circuit was not in use. |
| Simulated power-reactor heat removal | Reflux boiler. |
| Condenser cooling | Two circulation pumps, a standby-water-cooled heat exchanger and a surge tank. |
| Degassing branch | Small coolant flow; heater to dry steam for flow measurement; vessel capable of operation with hydrogen atmosphere. |
| Makeup and emergency supply | Pumps supplied distilled water from the storage tank. |
| Purification | Five parallel ion-exchange columns and filters. |

Source: [CNL 2022, p. 3-8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=148). “One of two” is stated for main circulation; the source does not assign every other pump pair the same duty/standby logic.

## Vent, drain and waste interfaces

The EIS routes vent and drain valves and bursting-disc/relief-valve outlets to the degassing vessel. A sampling/effluent pump transferred liquid to Building 200. These connections link pressure control, chemistry and waste handling. They do not identify the design pressure of the receiving tank, relief capacity or treatment efficiency. [CNL 2022, p. 3-8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=148).

The emergency supply was distilled water for this loop. It should not be assigned the organic injection fluid merely because nearby fast-neutron loops used WR-1's emergency organic system. Similarly, a hydrogen atmosphere in a loop vessel is not the helium differential-pressure arrangement used to support the main moderator.

## Applicability and missing information

The separator, reflux boiler and heater arrangement allowed controlled two-phase studies, but the public inventory supplies no common dated values for pressure, flow, heat input, quality, test-section diameter or measurement uncertainty. It also omits the full 1974 modification drawings and protective logic. Those details are required before identifying a particular experiment as representative of a commercial reactor condition.

The main WR-1 fuel-cooling velocity and chemistry ranges are not transferred to 1L2. Its primary fluid, phase behaviour, activity transport and purification system were different. The retained evidence establishes the facility's purpose and equipment path, while experiment-specific results need their own reports and effective operating inputs.
