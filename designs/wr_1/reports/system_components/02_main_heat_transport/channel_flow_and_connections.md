## Hydraulic function

The parallel channels needed an appropriate distribution of coolant, including when fuel or experimental hardware changed. The commissioning report describes replaceable flow and pressure-control orifices. CNL states that temporarily unfuelled positions received restrictors to give a flow equivalent to fuelled sites and preserve distribution. The sources do not provide the actual orifice sizes or a complete dated flow-allocation table. [AECL-2552, §6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=10); [CNL 2020, §2.7](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=10).

| Channel condition | Documented hardware response | Engineering implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ordinary fuelled channel | Feeder connections and flow/pressure-control restrictions | Channel flow depends on the complete parallel network, not only its bundle resistance. |
| Temporarily unfuelled site | Substitute flow restrictor | An empty position was not treated as an unrestricted bypass. |
| Experimental-loop site | Disconnect main-circuit inlet/outlet feeders and connect loop piping | Loop conditions and protective equipment require a separate basis. |
| Channel replacement | Removable joints; most feeders lacked isolation valves | Isolation and access formed part of maintenance planning. |

The first two implications are hydraulic interpretations of the stated equipment purposes. The loop conversion is explicitly described in [CNL 2020, §2.9.1](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

## Mechanical connections

The startup account lists 259 Grayloc seals in inlet/outlet feeders and also uses Graylocs at channel top closures. By the later operating account, at least 300 piping connections including closures were described. Minor leaks occurred, but the operators reported satisfactory general performance. Maintenance included correct bolt torque and progressive replacement of two-bolt Graylocs with four-bolt versions. These records do not supply joint drawings, torque values or acceptance criteria. [AECL-2552, §6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=10); [AECL-3523, §8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=14).

Most feeder valves were omitted to reduce potential leak points. The historical isolation technique for channel removal used frozen organic plugs. The public description establishes that the method was used; it does not provide enough information to reproduce a qualified isolation operation. [AECL-3523, p. 9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=14).

## Local-flow evidence

| WR-1 observation reported in 1980 | Interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Reference mean channel velocity approximately 9 m/s | A reference scale, not a guaranteed minimum between every pair of elements. |
| Long operation down to about 7.5 m/s without increased deposits | Favourable experience for the cited conditions and fuel. |
| Tests at about 3.0 and 4.8 m/s with increased fouling and some early failures | Lower average flow exposed a limitation in the tested cluster. |
| Source judged below 5 m/s unacceptable for the standard cluster with uncertain subchannel velocities | Geometry-specific conclusion; it is not a universal fluid-property threshold. |

Source: [AECL-6436, p. 51](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=58).

## Couplings and missing data

Changing a restriction changes channel flow and temperature rise; changes to the resulting coolant temperature can also change viscosity and degradation. Deposits can then alter both heat transfer and hydraulic resistance. This is a physically inferred feedback path consistent with the low-flow observations, not a quantified instability demonstrated by the sources.

No retrieved record establishes per-channel orifice coefficients, allowable flow errors, pressure taps, bypass leakage, bundle loss correlations or the final A/B/C connection map. A hydraulic calculation should therefore identify these as missing inputs rather than fit them implicitly from a single nominal velocity. The observed air ingress during early fuel changing also shows that operating and open-channel maintenance boundaries differ. [AECL-2552, §2.2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=7).
