## Circuit organization

The main path was circulating pump/flywheel, inlet headers and feeders, upward flow through assigned fuel channels, outlet feeders, and heat exchanger return. The arrangement let the reactor expose different channel groups to different coolant conditions. A channel connected to a separate experimental loop no longer followed the ordinary main-circuit feeder path. [1965 Fig. 1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=12); [CNL 2020, §2.9](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

| Circuit | Documented configuration |
| --- | --- |
| A and B | Initial independent circuits serving the 37 available sites; later described as identical in equipment, routing and allowable conditions. |
| C | Added in 1972; similar purpose but different equipment/circuit details. |
| Later heat-removal rating | Approximately 20 MWth per circuit through conventional shell-and-tube exchangers to river water. |
| Associated auxiliaries | Each circuit had its own coolant circulation, degassing and particulate-removal arrangement. |

Source: [CNL 2020, §§2.4–2.5](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=8). Functional circuit separation does not establish independence of electrical supplies, cooling-water sources or building services.

## Commissioning and service experience

The pump/motor base floated on springs. Its spring design used an 8,000-lb motor although the installed motor design had changed to 12,000 lb. Redesigning the spring support corrected the alignment/vibration problem after a reported 28-day delay. The lesson is specific: the pump, motor and support assembly needed a consistent mass and stiffness basis. [AECL-2552, §2.2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=7).

Opening a fuel channel initially drew air into the organic system, partly gas-locked pumps and caused unstable channel levels. Piping changes improved stability during fuel handling. This was a hydraulic and chemistry interface, not merely a refuelling inconvenience. [AECL-2552, §2.2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=7).

Early construction debris damaged seals, but the later account reported preventive seal changes only after 12,000–14,000 running hours following cleanup. The two observations concern different stages of system cleanliness. They do not define a universal seal replacement interval. [AECL-2552, §6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=10); [AECL-3523, §8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=14).

## Limits on reconstruction

The sources support the equipment path and dated operating scale. They do not supply complete pump curves, flywheel inertia, feeder dimensions, per-channel flow or heat-exchanger area. Consequently, rated motor power cannot be converted into a defensible circuit flow without pump efficiency and head, and flywheel presence alone cannot establish an adequate cooling duration after power loss.
