## Distinct monitored boundaries

| Monitoring function | Signal or location | What it addresses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Failed-fuel detection | Activity in coolant bleeds from outlet feeders | Release from fuel into organic coolant. |
| Main-circuit activity | Organic circuit activity | Broader contamination indication beyond individual feeder sampling. |
| Annulus leakage | Organic or heavy water in the CO₂ space | Failure of one of the separate channel/moderator boundaries. |
| Area radiation | External gamma field in occupied/work areas | Local radiation conditions; not a substitute for coolant sampling. |
| Ventilation monitoring | Activity/smoke in air pathways | Airborne contamination and fire indications. |

Sources: [AECL-3523, pp. 8–10](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=13); [CNL 2022, pp. 3-14, 3-16](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=154).

## Failed-fuel system and fuel dependence

The early system used Geiger tubes on channel coolant bleeds. The later retrospective inventory lists 19 Geiger detectors monitoring individual feeder activity and three monitoring the main circuits; an indication identified a suspected failure among three fuel rods. The grouped arrangement should not be restated as 53 simultaneously independent detector channels. The full switching or grouping diagram was not recovered. [CNL 2022, p. 3-16](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=156).

The 1973 conversion from UO₂ to UC required changes to the detection system because UC released smaller fission-gas signals. The 1980 account reports successful detection of the few UC defects discussed there, with low subsequent circuit activity. That experience is fuel- and event-specific. [AECL-6436, pp. 47–48](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=54).

## A demonstrated background limitation

Eight elements of an experimental uranium-impregnated graphite assembly ruptured in the early operating record. After gas purging, piping fields reached about 50 R/h and the failed-fuel system could not function against the background. Particulate cleanup and decay allowed operation to resume in approximately six weeks. This directly contradicts any assumption that low normal organic activity guaranteed available failure localization after a large release. [AECL-3523, §9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=15).

## Ventilation and filter experience

The active ventilation design directed air toward zones with higher contamination potential. The retrospective description divides it into building, cooling, control-room and extension systems with fans, filters, conditioning coils and exhaust paths, normally to a common stack. Local dampers balanced flow and pressure. [CNL 2022, §3.2.2.3](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=154).

Organic vapour shortened the life of the early absolute filters. The 1969 account describes bypassing them during normal operation with automatic insertion if ventilation activity rose; the author explicitly noted that the arrangement had not fully satisfied the safety reviewers and further development was underway. It is a historical unresolved issue, not a generally acceptable ventilation recommendation. [AECL-3523, §7](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=13).

## Limits

Detector thresholds, sampling delays, channel grouping, response to failed sample flow, filter efficiencies and ventilation pressure criteria were not recovered. The EIS's surviving AEP 2180 work-area monitor describes the decommissioning-era inventory; it does not establish the complete operating-era radiation network. Monitoring capability must be evaluated with fuel type, background, sample transport and equipment state, not detector count alone.
