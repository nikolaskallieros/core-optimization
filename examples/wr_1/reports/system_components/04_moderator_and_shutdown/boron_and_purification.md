## Reactivity function

Boron in the moderator adjusted reactivity to accommodate changing fuel burnup and poison conditions. The 1969 operating account describes semi-automatic adjustment through ion-exchange columns. Routine neutron-power regulation remained a moderator-level function, and rapid shutdown used moderator dumping. The published equipment descriptions do not establish this boron plant as an independent fast poison-injection shutdown system. [AECL-3523, p. 1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=6).

## Addition, removal and purity

The metering and mixing equipment supported controlled addition, while the operating account explicitly connected boron adjustment to ion exchange. The short public inventory does not map every valve path between the boron-removal function and the three mixed-bed moderator columns. A complete chemical-flow diagram should not be invented by assuming every listed column always served the same duty.

Moderator purification also has a different objective from isotopic purity. Removing ionic contamination does not, by itself, establish restoration of heavy water diluted with light water. Commissioning instead emphasized exclusion of residual ordinary water through drying and helium purging, and reported 99.78% isotopic content without measurable downgrading at that time. [AECL-2552, §2.1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=6).

The collection system accepted maintenance drains from moderator pumps, helium purge lines and sampling, with temporary connections for other equipment. Recovering that material helped manage a distinct heavy-water inventory rather than routing every drain automatically with general organic or process-water waste. [CNL 2020, p. 10](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=10).

## Interfaces and failure questions

| Interface | Engineering implication, not a quantified WR-1 failure result |
| --- | --- |
| Boron concentration to core | Changes neutron absorption and the moderator level needed for a given power/loading. |
| Metering and sample analysis | An unrepresentative sample or incorrect addition would affect the inferred poison inventory. |
| Ion-exchange state to reactivity | Resin selection, loading and flow routing can affect boron removal as well as impurity control. |
| Column contamination to maintenance | Shielded removal provisions indicate that radiation conditions could constrain service. |

The reviewed sources document functions and equipment but do not report a specific boron-system incident or a measured transfer function for these paths.

## Missing information

Boron chemical form, stock concentration, normal moderator range, addition/removal rates, resin types, column capacities, control logic and alarm setpoints were not recovered. The 2022 source also records decommissioning status, including removed resins; that later state does not describe what was installed during every operating year. No lifetime availability or cost estimate is assigned to the subsystem.
