## Function and hardware

The vertical pressure tubes contained hot organic coolant and the fuel string. Their extensions and end connections carried coolant through the upper and lower shield regions to the feeders. Readily removable channels enabled tests of alternative tube materials and dimensions. This experimental purpose was part of the original design, including periodic diameter measurement for irradiation-creep studies. [65-CNA-205, §4.1.4](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=7).

| Hardware | Function and evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Pressure tube | Organic pressure boundary around the fuel; separate from the aluminum moderator boundary. |
| Inlet and outlet connections | Joined the channel to its assigned primary circuit or experimental loop. |
| Top closure | Allowed access to the fuel string under shutdown conditions; Grayloc seals were used. |
| Removable feeder joints | Allowed changes to flow/pressure orifices, pressure channels and experimental-loop connections. |
| Monitored external annulus | Provided an indication of organic leakage outside the pressure channel. |

## Connections and service experience

Commissioning reported 259 Grayloc feeder seals, plus their use at the channel top closures. The 1969 account described at least 300 piping connections including closures. These statements have different dates and counting scopes. Minor leakage occurred, but the authors judged overall seal performance satisfactory. Two-bolt Graylocs were being replaced by four-bolt versions in the later account; correct bolt torque received explicit maintenance attention. [AECL-2552, §6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=10); [AECL-3523, §8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=14).

Most feeders omitted isolation valves to reduce potential leak sites. Channel removal used organic freeze plugs for isolation. This report records that historical design choice; the public account does not provide the qualified isolation procedure, plug verification requirements or permissible working conditions. A detected channel leak also demonstrates why good ordinary joint performance cannot be treated as a guarantee against pressure-boundary failures. [AECL-3523, pp. 9–10](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=14).

## Material degradation and evidence strength

Water and chlorine control helped maintain the protective zirconium oxide film and reduce hydrogen entry. The 1967 report estimated tube hydrogen from coupons on the assumption that the tubes absorbed hydrogen at the same rate. Its estimates of 13.6 ppm for Zircaloy-4 and 10.0 ppm for Zr–2.5Nb were therefore not direct whole-tube measurements. This distinction matters when interpreting apparent service life or allowable exposure. [AECL-2943, §3.1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=11).

Pressure, temperature, irradiation, chemistry and wall thickness jointly affect tube behaviour. The retained sources do not supply a complete stress analysis, creep history, defect acceptance criteria, end-fitting drawing or closure qualification. The initial 165-psia outlet pressure and later 350-lbf/in² pump description refer to different locations and dates; neither should be assigned as an unqualified channel design pressure.
