## Shield arrangement and functions

The original arrangement drawing places the radial thermal shield outside the calandria and identifies top and bottom shutdown shields around the axial access regions. CNL describes heat deposition in the shielding from nuclear radiation and thermal radiation from the primary system. The radial shield also supported rods suspending the calandria. These statements establish a combination of shielding, thermal and structural functions. [AECL-3523, Fig. I](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=7); [CNL 2020, pp. 8, 33](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=33).

The sources do not provide a complete dimensioned metal-layer stack. The labels “thermal shield,” “shutdown shield” and “biological shield” should therefore not be collapsed into one homogeneous material region for a radiation or thermal model.

## Cooling equipment and route

| Component or user | Documented arrangement |
| --- | --- |
| Circulation pumps | Two; one running and one standby. |
| Pump power | Motors connected to the Class III bus. |
| Heat exchanger | Two-bank shell-and-tube unit on the inlet line to the shields; heat ultimately rejected to the Winnipeg River. |
| Head vessel | Storage/deaerator head tank. |
| Cooling users | Radial shield, top and bottom shutdown shields, and ion-chamber-window cooling coils. |
| Auxiliary equipment | Piping, instrumentation and miscellaneous equipment; system associated with Room 504. |

Source: [CNL 2020, Table 1, p. 33](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=33). Unlike an inference from pump count alone, the one-running/one-standby arrangement and power class are expressly stated here.

## Relationship to other cooling systems

The thermal-shield loop was separate from the concrete-cooling loop and the heavy-water moderator circuit. The moderator removed heat deposited in the calandria and tubes, while the thermal-shield loop cooled the listed external shielding users. Both ultimately depended on a heat-rejection path, but the reviewed descriptions do not supply a complete common-header or isolation map. [CNL 2020, §§2.6 and Table 1](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=9); [CNL 2022, p. 3-15](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=155).

Inference: loss of shield cooling could change structural temperatures, support expansion and the temperature environment near ion-chamber windows. The available descriptions identify the physical connections but provide neither magnitudes nor demonstrated failure thresholds for those consequences. They do not show that the thermal-shield water was credited as direct emergency fuel cooling.

## Operating versus post-shutdown evidence

The 2020 characterization report states that thermal-shield and concrete-cooling systems were not fully drained and estimates up to 21 m³ of water remaining in the combined systems. That figure belongs to a later retained-water estimate; it is not the operating thermal-shield inventory or its emergency heat capacity. [CNL 2020, §6.8, p. 28](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=28).

## Remaining engineering data

Missing items include shield material grades and thicknesses, cooling passage geometry, branch flows, deposited heat by component, pressure/temperature limits, pump duties, changeover logic and exchanger performance. The sources reviewed here do not report a specific thermal-shield overheating incident. Absence of a reported event is not a reliability result.

A thermal or structural reconstruction would need both the shield geometry and the actual support arrangement, since the radial shield carried a vessel-support connection. A dose model would additionally need penetration and streaming details; bulk shield size alone cannot establish local fields.
