## Purpose

Examination required access to the sheath and support surfaces, which could be obscured by opaque irradiated organic film. CNL describes washing fuel in a xylene-filled converted storage tube before transfer to the hot cells. The smaller shielded flask carried fuel for examination, bundle shuffling, disassembly or waste packaging. [CNL 2020, §2.8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

The xylene wash tube and the wider organic wash-down system should remain distinct. The latter supplied/drained organic used in fuel handling and included equipment for encasing defective fuel. Similar names do not establish identical liquid routing or a single universal washing sequence.

## Shuffling and mechanical integrity

The 1980 review describes considerable experience shuffling bundles on their central support tubes partway through irradiation life. Hydrogen had migrated to endplates in some bundles, causing embrittlement and breakage. The author reports that design changes resolved the problem, but does not give the replacement drawings or a numerical handling-life qualification. [AECL-6436, p. 47](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=54).

This is a useful connection between in-reactor materials behaviour and later handling. An assembly that could still remove heat was not necessarily free of degraded support hardware. Conversely, the observed endplate problem does not establish that every fuel design or support alloy had the same susceptibility.

## Examination and associated storage

The original programme planned shielded metallurgical facilities capable of examining fuels and materials from WR-1 and Chalk River. That 1965 description is a construction/programme statement; later fuel transfers establish use of the Whiteshell hot-cell route, but neither source inventories every examination instrument or test method. [65-CNA-205, §4.1.6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=8); [CNL 2020, §2.8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

The EIS also describes a concrete-shielded, stainless-lined active dry-storage pit for irradiated or contaminated hardware, with an 8.34-tonne steel top plate and 27 stepped access holes. This was a hardware-storage facility; it is not substituted for the organic-filled fuel-storage arrangement. [CNL 2022, p. 3-13](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=153).

## Remaining evidence

Solvent inventory, wash effectiveness, exposure limits, defect-encapsulation design, bundle handling loads and individual examination results were not recovered in this system review. Cleaning moved coolant film and associated contamination into a liquid-waste route; it did not eliminate the material. The public equipment descriptions support that interface but not a quantitative solvent/activity balance or a complete hot-cell operating procedure.
