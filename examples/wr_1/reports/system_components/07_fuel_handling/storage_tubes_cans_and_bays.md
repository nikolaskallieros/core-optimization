## Two liquid inventories

Irradiated fuel was held in organic-filled tubes or cans surrounded by water. The organic maintained the fuel's immediate environment; the water provided external shielding and removed heat. This arrangement is not equivalent to placing bare UC fuel in a water pool. A spill of organic into the bay could foul the water, a concern recognized explicitly in the operating account. [CNL 2020, §2.8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11); [AECL-3523, §7](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=13).

## Storage bays and transfer conditions

Fuel commonly remained in the block for two to six weeks before moving to the storage bays. The described criterion was enough decay cooling to allow the dry portion of transfer without exceeding allowable sheath temperature. The report does not provide that temperature or a decay-heat limit. A calendar interval alone therefore cannot establish acceptability for every fuel history. [CNL 2020, §2.8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

At the receiving station, a can in the transfer trolley accepted the fuel. The can was filled with organic and immersed in bay water with its top above water. The spent-fuel-room crane moved it to storage racks. Work and storage bays were separated by stainless gates with inflatable seals, allowing drainage and maintenance; concrete surfaces had an epoxy lining with reinforced submerged joints. [CNL 2022, p. 3-11](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=151).

## Decay-heat removal and water treatment

The bay-water circuit pumped water from a change-volume tank through a shell-and-tube heat exchanger into the bottoms of the storage bays, work bay and block. Water returned by gravity. A small bypass passed through a sand/gravel filter and ion-exchange column; bay-water piping was stainless steel. Process water removed heat from the exchanger to the river. [CNL 2022, p. 3-13](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146078E.pdf#page=153); [CNL 2020, p. 11](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

The heat path is fuel → organic → container wall → bay water → exchanger → process water. This physical interpretation shows why organic level, container integrity and bay cooling are separate requirements. A cool bay does not by itself prove that every fuel item has the intended local heat-transfer path.

## Experience and missing data

The 1969 account reported no difficulty retaining organic in the storage tubes during the early period. That favourable observation is not a lifetime leak test or a storage safety analysis. CNL notes different downstream dispositions for UO₂ and UC, with UC retained in bays unless transferred to the hot cells. [AECL-3523, §7](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=13); [CNL 2020, §2.8](https://iaac-aeic.gc.ca/050/documents/p80124/146112E.pdf#page=11).

Rack spacing, can dimensions, working liquid depths, permissible load, cooling duty, loss-of-cooling response and criticality margins were not recovered. Those omissions prevent reconstruction of a qualified storage-loading plan from the equipment counts alone.
