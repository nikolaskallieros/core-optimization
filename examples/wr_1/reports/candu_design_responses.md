## Assessment and scope

**CANDU experience resolves the basic channel, refuelling, manufacturing and reactivity-control questions. It provides methods for managing hydrogen damage and ageing, but it does not qualify a new organic coolant, fuel bundle or partial-moderator-dump strategy.** The principal corrections are that low temperature does not eliminate irradiation damage, low pressure does not mean atmospheric pressure, and lower velocity can affect deposition independently of cooling.

Here, “water/water CANDU” means **heavy-water primary coolant and a separate heavy-water moderator** in conventional CANDU-PHW/CANDU 6. Light water is used in the secondary steam circuit. WR-1 used organic primary coolant and heavy-water moderator. Its vertical channels, shutdown refuelling and moderator-level regulation must not be confused with horizontal-channel CANDU 6 or the later vertical CANDU-OCR proposal. [C1, introduction and station flow diagram, PDF pp. 4–9](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/CANDU6_TechnicalSummary-s.pdf#page=4); [W4, pp. 1–3](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=6).

The sections below retain the numbering of the 15 questions. “Resolved” means the factual question or physical distinction is answered; it does not mean a proposed reactor has been designed or qualified. “Conditional” identifies a design choice whose feasibility depends on specified calculations or evidence.

## 1. Hydrogen embrittlement

**Disposition: the mechanism and management approach are established; an organic-service lifetime remains material- and chemistry-specific.**

Hydrogen absorbed by zirconium alloys can precipitate as hydrides as temperature and local solubility change. Distribution matters: a cooler attachment or local cold spot can accumulate hydrogen even when the average concentration looks acceptable. Organic-service tests showed strong dependence on the protective oxide, water concentration, chlorine species and temperature; simply making the coolant as dry as possible was not the successful strategy. [W6, pp. 3–5/PDF pp. 6–8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4442652#page=6).

CANDU water service has the related problem of deuterium uptake in pressure tubes. Its transferable approach is to track composition, dimensional changes, flaws and mechanical properties through inspection and fitness-for-service assessment. It does not provide a universal hydrogen limit for organic-cooled cladding. [C6, “How the CNSC ensures that pressure tubes are safe to operate”](https://www.cnsc-ccsn.gc.ca/eng/resources/fact-sheets/pressure-tubes/).

The cold-spot mechanism is particularly relevant to the two-tube arrangement. In normal CANDU operation, contact between a hot pressure tube and a cold calandria tube can form a local hydride blister; spacers and clearance monitoring help prevent that contact. [C7, abstract](https://www.cnsc-ccsn.gc.ca/eng/nuclear-safety-security/science-research-health/scientific-technical-information/technical-papers-and-articles/2018/regulatory-perspective-fitness-for-service/).

For an organic design, separate **fuel-cladding life** from **pressure-tube life**. They have different temperatures, stresses, irradiation histories and replacement schedules. The next required inputs are the chosen alloy/heat treatment, oxide condition, coolant chemistry and temperature/stress history. A chemistry specification plus local hydrogen and fracture assessment is needed; borrowing a water-reactor service life is insufficient.

## 2. Why a lower coolant-velocity limit?

**Disposition: correct the premise. The cited WR-1 summary rejected average velocities below approximately 5 m/s for its standard cluster; it did not establish a universal 3 m/s minimum.** Tests at 3.0 and 4.8 m/s produced more deposits and some premature failures. The summary does not give their power, heat flux or surface-temperature histories. Its surrounding design discussion explicitly considers restricting flow in proportion to lower channel power. [W5, §4.2.2.2, p. 51/PDF p. 58](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=58).

Two effects must be separated:

| Effect | What lower velocity can change | What the WR-1 summary establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heat transfer | Local coolant temperature, heat-transfer coefficient and cladding temperature, depending on simultaneous power changes and flow distribution. | Insufficient information to isolate this cause. |
| Deposition | Transport to the wall, contact/attachment and removal of fouling material. | An observed low-flow fouling problem, without a unique mechanism. |

For a clean channel, fixed properties/geometry and unchanged relative power/flow distributions, proportional reductions in power $P$ and mass flow $\dot m$ keep the bulk temperature rise $P/(\dot m c_p)$ constant, where $c_p$ is specific heat. With the illustrative turbulent scaling $h\propto v^{0.8}$, surface heat flux $q''\propto v$ gives a surface film drop $q''/h\propto v^{0.2}$ that actually decreases slightly. Here $h$ is the heat-transfer coefficient and $v$ the coolant velocity. This is an analytical comparison, not a reconstruction of the WR-1 tests. MIT organic-coolant data support this type of correlation within specified clean-surface conditions. [W8, §3.6](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/8338dd6b-c0e8-48bb-98a3-e61c2e4682d1/content#page=108).

Independent AECL paired tests outside the reactor found organic deposition of **0.8 versus 0.3 µg/(cm²·h)** at **3.1 versus 7.2 m/s**, with surface temperatures **484 versus 482°C**, during concurrent exposure to the same coolant. This supports a velocity effect separate from surface temperature. The proposed mechanism involves particle attachment and chemical bonding, whose probability depends partly on flow. However, the report's in-reactor X-706 comparison did not show a large velocity effect. [W7, §§4.3.1–4.3.3 and Table 15, PDF pp. 78–80](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4631967#page=78).

Higher flow can instead increase deposition associated with transport of dissolved iron compounds. In the paired example above, **total** deposition increased from **6.8 to 9.6 µg/(cm²·h)** despite the reduction in its organic portion. Thus lower velocity can promote one fouling mechanism without increasing every kind of deposit. [W7, Table 15 and conclusions, PDF pp. 80 and 85](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4631967#page=80).

**Remaining gap:** the original low-flow WR-1 irradiation records, including bundle identity, power and surface-temperature histories, chemistry and post-irradiation examinations. Another CANDU geometry cannot supply these missing experimental conditions.

## 3. Why pressurize a supposedly low-pressure coolant?

**Disposition: resolved. “Low pressure” is a comparison with water-reactor primary systems, not an assertion of atmospheric operation.**

| Source/configuration | Pressure stated | Interpretation |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| WR-1, 1965 operating-condition table | **165 psia ≈ 1.14 MPa absolute** at coolant outlet | About 11.4 bar absolute. The paired metric number printed in the original is inconsistent; this conversion uses the explicitly absolute psi entry. |
| CANDU 6, 2005 summary | **10.0 MPa** outlet-header pressure | A separate water-cooled reference, roughly an order of magnitude above the WR-1 value. |

Sources: [W1, Table 1, PDF p. 6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=6); [C1, heat-transport comparison, p. 15/PDF p. 18](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/CANDU6_TechnicalSummary-s.pdf#page=18).

Pressure keeps the liquid sufficiently above its local vapor-pressure condition and supports circulation without excessive gas formation or pump cavitation. Pump head must also overcome channel, feeder and exchanger losses. System absolute pressure, pump differential pressure and fuel-bundle pressure drop are distinct quantities. Mixture composition, dissolved gases and volatile removal affect the organic vapor pressure. [W3, §2.2, PDF p. 8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=8).

A future pressure specification therefore needs the actual hot-mixture properties, channel pressure profile and pump suction conditions. A low saturation pressure permits a lower-pressure design; it does not remove the pressure boundary.

## 4. Why both pressure tubes and calandria tubes?

**Disposition: resolved. They separate two fluids and two mechanical environments, with thermal insulation between them.**

| From the fuel outward | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Fuel elements and circulating coolant | Heat generation and primary heat removal. |
| Pressure tube | Contains primary coolant pressure and houses the fuel. |
| Gas annulus and spacers | Reduces heat leakage; maintains separation; allows leak monitoring. |
| Calandria tube | Separates the annulus from moderator and forms part of the moderator boundary. |
| Moderator | Provides neutron moderation and has its own circulation/heat-removal system. |

The CANDU 6 channel uses a zirconium-niobium pressure tube, concentric calandria tube and CO₂ annulus. WR-1's original calandria tubes were aluminum, so even the shared architecture does not imply identical material ageing. [C1, pp. 8–9/PDF pp. 11–12](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/CANDU6_TechnicalSummary-s.pdf#page=11); [W1, reactor description and Fig. 2](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=4).

The insulating gap is useful in normal operation but also limits heat transfer to the moderator if primary cooling is lost. Crediting the moderator as an emergency heat sink requires an accident heat-transfer model; see question 12. Normal-operation avoidance of tube contact and accident heat transfer after contact are different operating conditions.

## 5. What happens if fuel fails?

**Disposition: the possible progression is documented; there is no single outcome or universal release fraction.** Here, “fuel fails” means a breached fuel sheath, not automatically a ruptured pressure tube.

| Stage | Relevant consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Initial sheath breach | Fission gases or soluble species can enter coolant; coolant can enter the element depending on pressure differences and defect geometry. |
| Continued operation | Internal deposits, hydriding and mechanical deterioration can enlarge the defect. The initiating cause and subsequent damage need not be the same. |
| Circuit response | Activity may enter gas handling, purification equipment and deposited contamination; monitoring backgrounds can increase. |
| Fuel removal | The permissible delay depends on defect behavior, activity, coolability and handling integrity. |

An intentional SAP/UO₂ defect experiment developed internal carbonaceous material and progressive cladding damage. Later WR-1 assessment described internal hydriding after organic entry into defective zirconium-clad fuel. These results demonstrate routes to deterioration, not a common failure timetable. [W9, §§4–5, PDF pp. 25–33](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4797456#page=25); [W5, pp. 47–48/PDF pp. 54–55](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=54).

Fuel identity matters. UC exposed to water presents a different chemical problem from UO₂; the favorable behavior of selected UC defects cannot bound an unrelated uranium-graphite failure. WR-1 experienced an experimental-fuel event that substantially contaminated a circuit and impaired failed-fuel localization. [W4, pp. 10–11/PDF pp. 15–16](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=15).

CANDU's transferable provision is detection/localization followed by access to the affected channel; on-power fuelling can remove defective fuel when conditions permit. Organic qualification must additionally establish compatible cleanup, off-gas handling, fire behavior and defect-growth limits. The separation between sheath and channel boundaries remains essential.

## 6. How does on-power refuelling work? Does every channel have a valve?

**Disposition: resolved. WR-1 was refuelled while shut down. Water-cooled CANDU uses pressure-tight fuelling machines; opening a channel does not expose it to the reactor room.** [W4, p. 3/PDF p. 8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=8).

The operating principle is:

1. Position and lock a fuelling-machine head onto each channel end.
2. Establish the sealed connection and appropriate machine pressure, temperature and cooling flows.
3. Withdraw closure and shield plugs into the machines, extending the pressure boundary into their housings.
4. Insert new bundles from one end and receive displaced bundles at the other, maintaining fuel cooling.
5. Reinstall plugs, verify sealing and disconnect the machines.

The original Pickering design describes the pressurized magazine, header-referenced pressure control, storage positions for channel closures/shield plugs, and closure leakage testing after replacement. The machines themselves contain valves, locks and interlocks. This is a coordinated handling system, not a simple pair of channel shutoff valves. [C11, §§5.3.2 and 5.3.6.2, pp. 103 and 113–115/PDF pp. 111 and 121–123](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/OH-PickeringDD1969.pdf#page=121).

For WR-1 maintenance, the source explicitly says that its primary feeders had **no isolating valves**; freeze plugs isolated channels for pressure-tube replacement. That maintenance operation must not be described as on-power refuelling. [W3, §5.3, p. 11/PDF p. 15](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=15).

The commercial CANDU-OCR study proposed a different machine: top access to a vertical channel and exchange of a complete fuel string. It was a proposed design, not the refuelling arrangement operated in WR-1. [W5, fuel handling, p. 13/PDF p. 20](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=20).

## 7. Why start with degraded coolant, and how is it maintained?

**Disposition: resolved after separating a proposed startup mixture from actual WR-1 operation. HB-40 is a commercial coolant name; it does not mean “40% high boilers.”**

Santowax OM was solid at room temperature. Because WR-1 lacked complete trace heating, the designers considered a Santowax/high-boiler blend or a different liquid coolant. They chose fresh, partially hydrogenated HB-40. The earlier plan to replace HB-40 gradually with Santowax was superseded: the 1967 operating account made HB-40 the reference coolant. [W1, pp. 2–3](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=5); [W3, §2, p. 3/PDF p. 7](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=7).

Allowing degradation products to accumulate can be an economic operating choice: removing every altered molecule would increase processing and makeup. The acceptable mixture is determined by viscosity, heat transfer, volatility, chemistry and fouling. It is not acceptable merely because it has reached a chosen high-boiler percentage.

| Process | What it accomplishes |
| --- | --- |
| Degassing/condensation | Removes gases and controls volatile fractions. |
| Distillation | Separates selected heavy products and recovers reusable fractions. |
| Adsorption/filtration | Reduces particular fouling contaminants and particles; it does not reverse radiolysis. |
| Makeup and withdrawal | Maintains inventory and a specified composition range despite continued consumption. |

For example, the later OCR proposal allowed about **30 wt% high boilers** while continuously removing products and adding fresh OS-84. This is a maintained operating composition, not a chemically unchanged liquid. [W5, p. 11/PDF p. 18](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=18).

W3 reports decreasing decomposition rates as high-boiler concentration rose, but also continued volatile production and makeup requirements. A lower net rate does not establish zero damage or restoration of the original molecules. Question 15 separates this chemistry from circulation time.

## 8. Can pressure tubes be replaced? Do cold calandria components last indefinitely?

**Disposition: replacement is demonstrated; the claim that low temperature makes irradiation ageing unimportant is incorrect.**

WR-1 replaced two leaking pressure-tube assemblies, one with a weld defect and one with a leaking rolled joint. The historical account gives approximately **12 hours per tube, including fuel handling**, using feeder freeze plugs. This is a specific maintenance result, not a guaranteed outage duration for a different reactor. [W3, §5.3, PDF p. 15](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4557804#page=15).

OPG's Darlington refurbishment provides a larger-scale precedent: end fittings and fuel channels are removed with specialized tooling; **pressure tubes and calandria tubes are replaced**; the calandria vessel is cleaned, inspected and retained. Individual-channel maintenance and wholesale refurbishment have very different scope. [C5, fuel-channel removal, calandria-tube removal and reassembly](https://www.opg.com/projects-services/projects/nuclear/darlington-refurbishment/).

| Component | Correct lifetime interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Pressure tube | Replaceable component exposed to pressure, temperature, neutron irradiation, dimensional change and hydrogen-isotope uptake. |
| Calandria tube | Cooler, but irradiated and mechanically loaded; loss of ductility, joint behavior and sag still require assessment. Replacement is part of modern refurbishment practice. |
| Calandria vessel/end shields | Intended as long-lived structures; retention depends on material, weld, chemistry, fatigue and irradiation assessments, not temperature alone. |

IAEA documents irradiation strengthening and ductility loss in calandria tubes, joint stress relaxation and sag that can approach horizontal shutdown-injection nozzles. Its favorable assessment for the original design life is not a claim of indefinite life or evidence against later refurbishment replacement. [C4, §§4.1.5–4.1.6, 4.6 and 6–7, PDF pp. 36–44](https://www-pub.iaea.org/mtcd/publications/pdf/te_1197_prn.pdf#page=36).

The transferable design requirement is to preserve access, withdrawal clearances, replaceable connections, shielding and waste-handling routes from the outset. CANDU tooling establishes feasibility of the concept; it does not fit a new vertical organic channel without redesign.

## 9. Why a circular bundle, and can someone manufacture it?

**Disposition: circular channels have a structural basis beyond manufacturing convenience, and an established CANDU fuel supply chain exists.**

A circular pressure boundary carries internal pressure mainly through membrane stress. The thin-wall relation

$$
\sigma_\theta\approx\frac{\Delta p\,D}{2t}
$$

shows the dependence on pressure difference, diameter and wall thickness. A flat-sided channel develops bending and corner stresses as well; comparable performance generally needs a different structural solution. This is engineering reasoning, not a code-qualified tube design.

A roughly circular fuel envelope uses the round bore and passes through round closures and handling machines. Thermal-hydraulic performance still depends on rod sizes, spacers, peripheral gaps and local power. A circular envelope does not imply uniformly cooled rods or an optimal internal lattice.

Cameco manufactures CANDU UO₂ bundles and components; BWXT manufactures fuel pellets, zirconium-alloy tubes and assembled CANDU bundles. Thus “different from PWR fuel” does not mean “no manufacturing precedent.” [C9](https://www.camecofuel.com/business/cameco-fuel-manufacturing); [C10](https://www.bwxt.com/bwxt-nec/nec-faqs/).

That evidence does **not** establish current availability of WR-1 fuel, a UC production line, an enriched-fuel licence, organic-compatible cladding, or an economical order at a particular volume. The practical procurement baseline is an existing CANDU bundle family; any changed fuel material, sheath, support scheme or chemistry requires supplier assessment and qualification. No supplier has been contacted and no quotation obtained.

## 10. Could moderator level really provide fine control without control rods?

**Disposition: yes. WR-1 used helium differential pressure to regulate moderator level, with boron addition/removal for slower compensation.** The commissioning report describes stable neutron-power regulation, while separately reporting difficulties with thermal-power instrumentation. These are evidence of an operating control method and its actual limitations. [W4, p. 1/PDF p. 6](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=6); [W2, §2.3, PDF p. 8](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=8).

Changing level changes reflector thickness, neutron leakage and, at greater withdrawal, the effectively moderated region. Fine movement can therefore make small reactivity changes without a solid absorber moving between fuel rods.

The limitation is spatial control. A single moderator level is one global actuator; it cannot independently correct several regional power errors. Ontario Hydro's training material explicitly identifies flux distortion and the absence of zone control as disadvantages of level regulation. [C2, lesson 227.00-13, PDF p. 161](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/20050600.pdf#page=161).

The next design question is therefore required control authority and spatial power regulation, not whether rodless regulation is physically possible. A small research core and a large power-producing core need not choose the same actuators.

## 11. Would grey rods simplify operation? How fast is moderator dumping?

**Disposition: absorbers are a credible alternative, but “easier” must include the entire control system and its effects on power shape.**

| Function | Relevant CANDU precedent | Implication for an organic design |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fine global/spatial regulation | Distributed liquid-zone absorbers | Keep the bulk moderator inventory while adjusting regional absorption. |
| Flux shaping/reactivity reserve | Adjuster rods; supplementary mechanical absorbers | Evaluate normal positions, operating range, local peaking and parasitic absorption. |
| Rapid shutdown | Separate shutoff rods and liquid-poison injection in later water CANDUs | Shutdown speed, independence and available worth require their own demonstration. |

These functions are described separately in the CANDU reference material. A grey regulating rod is not automatically a qualified shutdown device. Fabricating its absorbing body is only part of the job: guides, drives, penetrations, position sensing, failure behavior and core-power effects also matter. [C1, reactivity control and shutdown, PDF pp. 27–28 and 49–50](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/CANDU6_TechnicalSummary-s.pdf#page=27).

WR-1's commissioning table reports **15.5 mk removed during the first second of moderator dumping**, compared with a 12.5 mk design value. This is measured early shutdown worth for the commissioning configuration, not a complete emptying time or a universal shutdown rate. [W2, Table I, PDF p. 9](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=9).

Scale matters. The CANDU designers' historical account explains that moderator dumping was adequate for NPD and Douglas Point, but its early reactivity removal was insufficient for some large-break accidents in the larger Pickering A core, motivating mechanical shutoff rods. Later designs adopted diverse, independent shutdown systems. [C3, pp. 2–3](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/20010305.pdf#page=2).

The later CANDU-OCR proposal already moved toward light-water zone control, absorbers, liquid shutoff rods and poison injection. It is a useful design alternative to assess before committing to moderator dumping for a new organic reactor. [W5, pp. 9–10/PDF pp. 16–17](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=16).

## 12. Could we dump only enough moderator to become subcritical and retain cooling?

**Disposition: physically conceivable, but a safe stopping height is not established. Retaining moderator and ensuring shutdown are separate requirements.**

The concern about losing the heat sink is valid. Heat must traverse the fuel/coolant region, pressure tube and annulus before reaching a calandria tube. Dumping the surrounding liquid changes that tube's cooling boundary; its temperature cannot simply remain fixed in the model. Shutdown also leaves decay heat and stored thermal energy to remove.

Water-CANDU accident studies can credit moderator heat absorption after a hot pressure tube balloons or sags into its calandria tube, **provided adequate local moderator cooling preserves channel integrity**. The cited analysis explicitly assesses moderator subcooling as this heat enters the liquid. This is a conditional accident mechanism, not the normal insulating-gap heat-transfer path. Its timing and capacity do not transfer automatically to WR-1's vertical channels, aluminum calandria tubes or organic-filled channels. [C8, slides 17–22](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/19990116.pdf#page=17).

WR-1 commissioning itself demonstrates why a single geometric level is inadequate: the reported clean critical level changed from **84.45 in for 19 fuelled sites to 59.5 in for 25 sites**. Those levels use the source's datum and commissioning states; neither is a shutdown limit. [W2, Table I](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4513855#page=9).

To qualify partial dumping, a coupled assessment must establish:

1. Negative reactivity versus level **and time**, across fuel loading/burnup, temperature, poison, coolant-void and credible failure states, with the specified shutdown margin and uncertainty treatment.
2. Peak fuel and tube temperatures while the moderator surface falls, including which portions become uncovered and how much liquid remains available to absorb heat.
3. Reliable dump arrest, prevention of unintended refilling, and continued heat removal from the retained inventory to an ultimate sink.

An instantaneous $k_\mathrm{eff}<1$ at one nominal state does not establish adequate transient protection or long-term shutdown. **The CANDU-informed candidate worth evaluating first is to retain the moderator and use dedicated absorbers/poison for shutdown**, together with separately demonstrated decay-heat removal. This is a candidate architecture, not a completed selection.

## 13. Which water CANDUs used moderator-height control or moderator dumping?

**Disposition: there are direct precedents, but they changed with reactor generation.**

| Reactor/generation | Regulation relevant to this question | Shutdown relevant to this question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| WR-1 | Moderator level plus soluble boron compensation | Moderator dump. |
| NPD | Moderator level was the primary automatic regulating method | Moderator dump. |
| Douglas Point | Mechanical control absorbers; moderator level as secondary control | Moderator dump. |
| Original Pickering A | Liquid-zone control; moderator level as secondary control | Shutoff rods with moderator-dump backup; these were not wholly independent systems. |
| Later water-cooled CANDUs, including CANDU 6 | Zone control with other reactivity devices and on-power fuel management | Separate solid shutoff rods and rapid moderator poison injection. |
| Proposed commercial CANDU-OCR | Zone control and absorbers | Liquid shutoff rods and moderator poison injection. |

Sources: [W4, p. 1](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4114552#page=6); [C2, Table 3, PDF p. 170](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/20050600.pdf#page=170); [C3, pp. 2–3](https://canteach.candu.org/Content%20Library/20010305.pdf#page=2); [W5, pp. 9–10](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=16). The historical rows describe the cited configurations, not every subsequent plant modification. In particular, Table 3 also lists a booster rod for NPD's xenon override: primary regulation by moderator level did not mean the absence of every rod-based reactivity device.

Shared features include pressure channels, a separate cool moderator, compact bundles and accessible channel ends. Coolant chemistry, pressure, orientation, fuel composition and reactivity feedbacks remain design-specific. CANDU precedent answers how a function can be implemented; it does not establish the organic reactor's coefficients or protection margins.

## 14. Should we compare circular, square or triangular fuel arrangements at the burnup limit?

**Disposition: yes, but first separate three different geometry choices. A burnup ceiling alone does not determine the best arrangement.**

| Geometry choice | What changes | Consequential comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pressure-channel cross-section | Structural shell, closures, end fittings and handling interface | Pressure stress, material quantity, replaceability and manufacturability. |
| Rod arrangement within a channel | Rings, square pitch, triangular pitch, rod count/diameter and peripheral gaps | Local power, surface heat flux, mixing, deposition, pressure loss and fuel support. |
| Channel lattice in the moderator | Square versus triangular channel centers, pitch and reflector | Moderation, neutron leakage, device access and core size. |

These choices can be combined. A circular tube can contain different rod arrangements, and circular bundles can occupy a square channel lattice. WR-1's original channel lattice was triangular; the commercial OCR design used a square lattice. Neither statement specifies the lattice of rods inside one bundle. [W1, Fig. 2/PDF p. 13](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4891392#page=13); [W5, p. 8/PDF p. 15](https://inis.iaea.org/records/e4zqc-d2129/files/11559681.pdf#page=15).

A useful analytical screen illustrates the trade. At fixed total fuel cross-sectional area $A_f$, active length $L$ and bundle power $P$, with $N$ equal cylindrical rods and approximately uniform rod power:

$$
d_f=\sqrt{\frac{4A_f}{\pi N}},\qquad
q'_{\rm rod}=\frac{P}{NL},\qquad
q''\propto N^{-1/2}.
$$

The last scaling treats heated diameter as proportional to fuel diameter and neglects sheath/gap corrections. More smaller rods can reduce linear power and surface heat flux, but increase surface/structural complexity; available coolant passages, local flow and neutron absorption must be recalculated. It is not a prediction of improved burnup or fouling.

The first useful comparison is therefore **alternative rod arrangements inside the same circular pressure-channel envelope**. Hold fuel material/enrichment, coolant specification, active length, target power and required discharge burnup fixed; state whether coolant flow or pump head is imposed, rather than fixing both independently. Compare peak rod power, surface temperature with deposits, channel pressure loss, irradiation deformation, handling clearances and manufacturing changes. Only then is it informative to consider changing the pressure-boundary shape.

The current evidence supports this comparison strategy, not a winning geometry. The actual burnup target, power, channel dimensions and chosen fuel/cladding are missing; no geometry optimization was performed.

## 15. Does faster circulation reduce degradation? Does coolant recover outside the core or saturate?

**Disposition: shorter exposure per pass does not, by itself, reduce the average radiation dose rate of a recirculating inventory. Leaving the core is not a regeneration process.**

For an illustrative well-mixed loop, hold core and total coolant masses, radiation field, density and thermal state fixed. Let $M_c$ be irradiated coolant mass, $M_L$ total loop mass, $\dot m$ circulation rate and $\dot D_c$ the representative in-core absorbed dose rate. Core transit time $t_c$, dose per pass $D_{\rm pass}$ and mean pass frequency $f_{\rm pass}$ then satisfy

$$
t_c=\frac{M_c}{\dot m},\qquad
D_{\rm pass}=\dot D_c\frac{M_c}{\dot m},\qquad
f_{\rm pass}=\frac{\dot m}{M_L},
$$

$$
\overline{\dot D}=D_{\rm pass}f_{\rm pass}
=\dot D_c\frac{M_c}{M_L}
=\frac{P_{\rm absorbed}}{M_L}.
$$

Here $P_{\rm absorbed}$ is radiation energy deposited in the coolant per unit time, **not total reactor thermal power**. Faster circulation decreases dose per pass but increases the number of passes; the circulation rate cancels under these assumptions. This is an inventory balance, not a kinetic degradation correlation.

Flow can still change real degradation through temperature distribution, stagnant regions, the timing of irradiation and subsequent reactions, mixing and treatment exposure. If reactor power changes with flow, the radiation field is no longer fixed, so the cancellation does not assert unchanged damage. MIT's work also shows that irradiated mixtures can continue decomposing thermally outside the radiation field; cooler or unirradiated piping does not restore virgin terphenyl. [W8, abstract, thermal-decomposition discussion and maintained-composition experiments](https://dspace.mit.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/8338dd6b-c0e8-48bb-98a3-e61c2e4682d1/content#page=5).

For a maintained inventory, a conceptual high-boiler balance is

$$
M_L\frac{dw_H}{dt}
=R_{H,\rm net}-\dot m_{H,\rm separation}-w_H\dot m_{\rm purge},
$$

Here $w_H$ is the high-boiler mass fraction, $R_{H,\rm net}$ its net mass production rate, $\dot m_{H,\rm separation}$ the separated high-boiler mass flow and $\dot m_{\rm purge}$ the mixed-coolant purge flow. Net production includes formation and further destruction of heavy products; the expression assumes constant inventory and makeup with negligible high-boiler content. A steady $w_H$ means these rates balance. It does not mean all chemical reactions stop, that viscosity/fouling remain acceptable, or that no makeup is consumed. Radical recombination is part of the chemistry, not a demonstrated plant-scale reversal of accumulated products.

**Remaining quantitative dependency:** the selected coolant's dose-rate/temperature/composition-dependent yields, irradiated and hot inventory fractions, and actual cleanup/makeup flows. Water-CANDU mechanical experience cannot supply these organic-chemistry parameters.
